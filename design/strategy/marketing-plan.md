# Marketing plan: one funnel the whole team moves on

Written 2026-10-26 by the marketing specialist role. Inputs: `social-media.md`, `growth-angle.md`, `learning.md` and `roadmap.md`. Where the three reports agree, this plan says so once and moves on. Where they differ or leave a gap, it decides and says why.

**Where they agree.** All three land on the same product: the calm answer to "something just happened," built as a free checker whose output is a card people send to family or post where the losses start. Facebook first, LinkedIn second from a named person, no social until the product exists, no fear-selling, no detection claims, every number sourced. The Path stays the house; the checker is the door. This plan builds on that agreement and resolves four gaps: which comes first (artifacts or checker), the order of the first verdicts, what the shareable phrase is, and whether the free AI rule stays behind an account.

## 1. Positioning

**Positioning statement.** Data Hygienics is the independent, plain-English guide that puts a home's or a small business's digital life in order, so technology helps instead of worrying them. When something feels off, it is the calm place to check. We sell nothing but our own business tools, we source every number, and we start with what you'd hate to lose.

**The enemy.** Complexity and fear-selling. Every competitor in the panic moment either reads like a government form or ends in a product pitch. We are against both, out loud: "Here's the whole business model" is a post, not a footnote.

**The phrase people repeat.** There are two, for two moments, and that is deliberate:

- In the panic moment: **"Hang up. Call back on a number you already have."** It is an if-then rule, it needs no product, it works however good the fake gets, and it fits on a card. This is the line on every share card.
- In the setup moment: **"Start with what you'd hate to lose."** It is the front door and the Path. This is the line under the mark.

Decision: the share card carries the first phrase; the site headline stays "Your digital life, in order." with the second as the chip prompt. Do not put the brand slogan on the card. Cards travel when they carry a behavior, not a brand.

## 3. The funnel

Six stages, one metric each. Nothing else is a target.

1. **Arrive.** Search for the exact message type, or a card shared on Facebook, a LinkedIn carousel, a handoff from an accountant or a library. Metric: visits that land on a verdict, step or artifact page (not the homepage).
2. **Answer.** The verdict page or the front door gives the two-sentence answer or the first chip. Metric: share of those visits that reach the "next two minutes" block or click a chip.
3. **Act.** The first Path step, shown immediately after a chip, with its done ritual. Metric: steps marked done within 24 hours of first visit.
4. **Keep.** The path saved to a member account (magic link, already built). Metric: saved paths.
5. **Return.** The monthly note on the first of the month: what changed, and whether your path changed. Metric: return visits within seven days of the note.
6. **Pay (business only).** Free Quick Check, free AI rule, then the Documentation Pack. Metric: packs bought, and the share that came through a verdict or the AI rule.

A seventh number sits across all of them: prints and card downloads. It is the growth engine, because a card on a fridge recruits without a post.

## 4. Message hierarchy by page type

The learning rules in force on every page: end state first, threat named once, action, an if-then sentence, a "set once" or "ongoing" label, and one observable check.

**Homepage.** Headline (Your digital life, in order). One line of what we are. The chips. The promise. Nothing else above the promise. The "Something just happened?" door lives in the navbar on every page, not as a homepage section.

**Verdict page** (`/check/<slug>`). Title as the message people typed ("A text says I owe a toll"). Two-sentence verdict, present tense, no hedging ("This is almost always a scam. Real toll agencies don't text for payment."). "The next two minutes": three numbered actions. "Who to tell": the bank line on the card, ReportFraud.ftc.gov, ic3.gov, each one line. "Say this": the if-then sentence. The share card. "Make sure it can't happen again": one Path chip. Then, below the fold, why this works, the dated figures and the sources, and "reviewed on." No comments, no statistics above the fold, no vendor names.

**Step page** (`/steps/<id>`, and the step card inside the Path). Title as the action. "After this:" one sentence. Why it holds, threat named once. Label: set once or ongoing, minutes. Do, three to five items. Say this. Check. The newcomer note. Links below the fold. The four tellings stay as views of the same step.

**Hub** (`/household`, `/business`, `/digital-sovereignty`). One line that names the end state for this audience. The three "start here" items. Then the library sections as they are. The hub is the browse layer, so it may be long, but its first screen is three things, not seven.

**Artifact page** (`/cards/<slug>`: the safe-word card, the card by the phone, the one-page AI rule, the "we never change payment details by email" notice). The card itself, large. One line on how to use it ("Put it where the phone is."). Print and download. The step it belongs to. Its own social card image so a shared link shows the artifact, not the site logo.

## 5. The hero product brief

**Name candidates.** "Is this real?" (the question people type; recommended), "The Two-Minute Check," "Something just happened." Recommendation: the public name is the question, **Is this real?**, at `/check`, with each verdict at `/check/<slug>`. The navbar door reads "Something just happened?" because that is the state of the person clicking it; the page they land on asks "Is this real?" The owner decides the name (open decision 1); the structure below holds for any of them.

**What it is.** One screen, one question: "What just happened?" A grid of chips, each a message type in the reader's words. Each chip is a verdict page, indexable, with its own title, social card and sources. The checker makes no detection claim, ever. It gives a procedure.

**The first six verdicts, in priority order, with the reason.**

1. **A text says I owe a toll.** Government-imposter reports rose 40% in 2025, driven by toll texts spoofing EZ-Pass, SunPass, FasTrak and TxTag (FTC consumer alert, May 2026). The top results are vendor blogs. Year-round, and the exact-message search is enormous.
2. **A text or call says it's my bank's fraud department.** Bank-impersonation texts up nearly twentyfold since 2019 with a median loss of $3,000 (FTC Data Spotlight, 2023). Highest stakes of the six, and the verdict is the brand's signature rule: hang up, call the card.
3. **A call sounded like my child or grandchild and asked for money.** The site already owns this (voice cloning threat, safe-word guide). The honest verdict is procedure, not detection, which is the position vendor blogs cannot take. This is the verdict the adult child shares to a parent's timeline.
4. **I clicked a link, or gave information, and I'm not sure what happens now.** "Did I get hacked" has no calm, ordered answer outside Reddit. This verdict sequences: change the password from a different device, add a passkey, check recovery details, freeze credit if an ID was shared, report. It hands straight into the Accounts stage.
5. **A text says a package couldn't be delivered.** Among the top five reported text scams (FTC Data Spotlight, 2023); peaks November and December, which is now. Ships because it is cheap to write once the toll verdict exists, and it catches the holiday spike.
6. **A vendor emailed new bank details** (business). Business email compromise cost $3.05 billion across 24,768 complaints in 2025 (FBI IC3 2025 Annual Report). The verdict is the two-channel rule the site already has, and it hands into the free AI rule and the Documentation Pack. It is the first business door and the first LinkedIn carousel.

Seventh, with a hard date: **The IRS called or texted me**, live by January 5, 2027. IRS-impersonation calls rose 400% between January and late February 2026 (Joint Economic Committee alert, April 9, 2026). The growth report puts it in the first wave; this plan puts package text ahead of it only because the holiday spike arrives before tax season does. Both must exist before January.

Business verdicts that follow: "someone on my team pasted client data into a chatbot," "my boss texted asking for gift cards," "do I have to report this breach."

**Verdict page structure.** See section 4. The one rule that matters most: the verdict sentence comes first and is never hedged into uselessness. "Almost always a scam" is honest and useful; "it could be a scam" is neither.

**The share card.** A 1200 by 630 image and a printable half-page, generated per verdict. Contents: the message type in one line ("A text about an unpaid toll?"), the rule in one line ("Hang up. Call back on a number you already have." or the verdict's own if-then), the mark, and the short URL. No statistics on the card, no dollar figures, nothing that reads as alarm. The card is a gift, so the share control reads "Send this to someone you look out for" with a pre-written calm message, not a bare share icon. Every artifact page uses the same card system.

**How each verdict hands into the Path.** The last block on every verdict is "Make sure it can't happen again," with one chip that starts the Path with that answer chosen (`/path/home?start=money` for the bank and toll verdicts, `?start=people` for the family-voice verdict, `?start=accounts` for the clicked-link verdict, `/path/business?start=payments` for the vendor verdict). The learning rule holds: the first step shows immediately, and the first step offered is the easiest related one (the bank-callback rule, the safe word), not the most important.

**Independence and voice, absolute.** No product is named on a verdict. When a tool would help, the verdict links to the Path step, where the toolkit entry sits beside a free option as it does today. No "detect," "spot," or "identify" claims anywhere. Every figure above the fold is pushed below it; every figure carries a named, dated source and the page shows "reviewed on."

## 6. The launch sequence

**Wave 0: the product exists.** Nothing posts until all of these are true.
- The checker at `/check` with the six verdicts, their share cards and per-page social cards.
- First-click-pays on the Path, so a chip on a verdict lands on a step.
- Artifact pages for the safe-word card and the card by the phone, with print.
- Per-page Open Graph titles and images for every guide, threat, step and verdict.
- A named author with a face and a why on `/about`, and bylines on verdicts.
- Vercel Web Analytics on, Search Console and Bing verified, the sitemap resubmitted, and the four events wired (chip clicked, path completed, step done, path saved).

**Wave 1: Facebook, alone, for one month.** Two posts a week from the page, one scam and one habit, each pointing at a verdict or a card. One reply session a week. Encourage sharing into family group chats; run no group. The learning report's rule for every post: the rule first, the threat in one sentence, the card.
- Must be true first: Wave 0, plus the IRS and package verdicts live if the month includes December or January.

**Wave 2: LinkedIn from the founder's profile, and the business lane.** One document carousel a week walking through one business step or verdict, reshared by the page, plus comments on five posts a week from accountants, practice managers and IT providers. The vendor-bank-details verdict leads, then the free AI rule.
- Must be true first: the business verdicts, indexable step pages for the business Path, and the decision on the AI rule's account wall (open decision 2). A carousel that ends at a sign-up form will lose the reader; the growth report is right that the rule should be free to read.

**Wave 3: Instagram cross-posts, the monthly note, and the member account named.** Reels cut from the Facebook material; the first monthly note on the first of the month after the member account has a name; honest social proof ("most households start with the safe word") once the analytics say so and not before.
- Must be true first: thirty days of Facebook data showing which cards travel, the note's template, and the member account naming decision (open decision 3).

X is a listening channel throughout: replies to researchers and journalists, corrections, and the original study when it exists. No audience-building effort.

## 7. Thirty-day calendar skeleton, Wave 1 (Facebook)

Two posts a week, Tuesday and Friday, plus a reply session on Thursday. Each post names its landing page. Sequence assumes a start in the first week of a month, which the fresh-start evidence favors.

| Week | Tuesday (the scam, as a verdict) | Friday (the habit or artifact) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "A text about an unpaid toll?" The verdict in two lines, the toll share card. Lands on `/check/toll-text`. | The safe word. "Pick a word tonight." The printable card. Lands on the safe-word artifact page. |
| 2 | "Your bank's fraud department is calling." The rule: hang up, call the card. Lands on `/check/bank-fraud-call`. | The card by the phone. Photo of a real fridge. Lands on the artifact page. |
| 3 | "A package couldn't be delivered." Holiday timing. Lands on `/check/package-text`. | "What we cut," first entry: deepfake detector apps, and why a safe word doesn't depend on spotting anything. Lands on the Path's What we cut. |
| 4 | "A call that sounded like your grandson." The procedure, no detection claim, the family card. Lands on `/check/family-voice-call`. | "Here's the whole business model." The promise post. Lands on `/how-it-works`. |

Thursday reply sessions: answer in the thread with the rule and the card, never ask people to describe what happened to them in public (Meta's own guidance, October 2025), and point anyone who has lost money to the bank line and ReportFraud.ftc.gov in a private message.

Week 5 onward alternates the remaining verdicts with the step series ("one step, four ways") and repeats the safe word monthly with a different family in the photo. The IRS verdict takes the first Tuesday of January.

## 8. Open decisions for the owner

1. **The checker's name.** "Is this real?" at `/check` is the recommendation. The navbar door reads "Something just happened?" either way.
2. **The AI Acceptable Use Policy's account wall.** Today it is free with a free account. The growth and social reports both say the wall costs the business lane its best door. Recommendation: free to read and print with no account; the account is for saving and customizing. This changes the funnel's "Pay" stage and needs a yes.
3. **The named face.** Facebook trusts a neighbor and LinkedIn rewards a person. Someone's name, photo and why go on `/about`, on verdict bylines and on the LinkedIn carousels. Recommendation: the founder, and the About page says why this exists.
4. **The member account's name,** and with it whether "members" is the word on the site. Needed before Wave 3 and the first monthly note. Options worth testing in copy: "your path," "members," "the household." Recommendation: no name on the account at all until the note has gone out twice; the note earns the name.
5. **Instagram at all in the first quarter.** The audience is there (64% of 30 to 49, Pew, October 2026), but it is a third channel for one person. Recommendation: cross-post only, no Instagram-first content, and revisit after Wave 1's data.

Everything else in this plan is decided. The team should move on Wave 0 now.
