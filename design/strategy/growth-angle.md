Written 2026-10-26 by the growth strategist role. Inputs: the site inventory,
the owner's vision (calm club, deep not wide, secure what you have then add

## 1. The demand map

The traffic in this category comes from two moments: the panic moment ("is
this real, what do I do right now") and the setup moment ("how do I protect
X"). Panic moments are far larger and far worse served. Government pages
answer them in bureaucratic prose, vendor blogs answer them with a product
pitch, and Reddit answers them well but slowly and unreliably.

### Why the panic moment is the market

- The FTC's Consumer Sentinel logged about 3 million fraud reports and a record $15.9 billion in reported losses in 2025, up from $12.5 billion in 2024 (FTC press release, March 25, 2026, and FTC testimony to the Joint Economic Committee, 2026).
- Imposter scams were the most-reported category: more than 1 million reports and $3.5 billion lost in 2025, up nearly 20 percent. Government-imposter reports rose 40 percent, driven partly by overdue-toll texts spoofing EZ-Pass, SunPass, FasTrak and TxTag (FTC consumer alert "New trends in reports of imposter scams," May 2026).
- Social media was the costliest contact method in 2025 at $2.1 billion, eight times the 2020 figure, and Facebook was the top platform by losses (FTC Data Spotlight, April 2026). Facebook is both where losses start and where families share warnings. That is the distribution channel.
- By report count, the contact methods for 2025 were website or app 31 percent, social media 28 percent, phone 11 percent, email 10 percent, text 7 percent (FTC Data Spotlight, April 2026). Texts are a small share of reports but a huge share of "is this real?" searches, because a text arrives with no context.
- People 50 and older reported $4.3 billion lost in 2025 versus $2.3 billion for younger adults (FTC, 2026). The FBI counted $7.7 billion in losses reported by people 60 and older in 2025, up 59 percent (FBI IC3 2025 Annual Report, p. 44, already cited on the site).
- AARP's Fraud Watch helpline takes around 500 calls a day (Kathy Stokes, testimony to the Senate Special Committee on Aging, January 14, 2026). That is roughly 180,000 panic moments a year reaching one phone line. Searches dwarf that.
- Tax season is a reliable spike: the FTC told the AP on March 16, 2026 that robocalls, texts and phishing were up this filing season, and the Joint Economic Committee's April 9, 2026 alert cited a 400 percent rise in IRS-impersonation calls between January and late February 2026. The IRS 2026 Dirty Dozen leads with impersonation (IRS release, 2026).

### Home intents (highest volume, weakest coverage)

| Intent, as typed | Why it is under-served |
|---|---|
| "Is this toll text a scam" (EZ-Pass, SunPass, FasTrak, TxTag) | FTC alert is generic; the top results are vendor blogs. Nobody gives the two-minute procedure. |
| "USPS / FedEx package text scam" | Same pattern. Secondary sources call fake delivery texts the most-reported text scam (unverified vendor claim; FTC confirms delivery texts among the top five, Data Spotlight 2023). |
| "Text from my bank fraud department real or scam" | FTC found bank-impersonation texts up nearly twentyfold since 2019 with a median loss of $3,000 (FTC Data Spotlight, 2023). High stakes, thin coverage. |
| "Grandchild called needs bail money" / "voice sounded like my son" | Dominated by detection-product blogs. The honest answer is procedure, not detection: hang up, call back on a known number. The site already owns this (safe word and callback rule). |
| "Did I get hacked" / "I clicked a link what do I do" | No calm, ordered answer exists outside Reddit. |
| "I gave a scammer my info / sent money, what now" | FTC and IC3 reporting pages exist, but no triage that sequences bank, freeze, report. |
| "IRS called me" (January through April) | Seasonal, enormous, and the IRS page is a wall of text. |
| "Facebook Marketplace scam" / "Zelle scam" | Facebook is the top loss platform (FTC, April 2026). Coverage is forum threads. |
| "Is this job offer a scam" | Rising imposter category; weak coverage. |
| "Gift card scam what to do" | Government pages exist; nothing shareable to hand a parent. |

### Business intents (lower volume, higher value, almost no calm coverage)

| Intent, as typed | Why it is under-served |
|---|---|
| "Vendor changed bank details email real" / "wire instructions changed" | Business email compromise cost $3.05 billion across 24,768 complaints in 2025, second only to investment fraud (FBI IC3 2025 Annual Report). Results are insurance and vendor blogs. |
| "Employee pasted client data into ChatGPT what do we do" | 77 percent of AI users paste data into chatbots and 82 percent of pastes come from personal accounts (LayerX browser telemetry, 2025). 45 percent of employees used AI on work devices in Verizon's 2026 DBIR data, two-thirds via non-corporate accounts (DBIR 2026 Executive Summary, p. 12, already cited on the site). Nobody answers the "it already happened" version. |
| "AI acceptable use policy template small business" | Plenty of templates, none free, short and explained. The site's free AI policy is the right product, under-promoted. |
| "Boss texted asking for gift cards" | Classic CEO fraud, usually answered by vendor blogs. |
| "Do I have to report a breach" (small business, by state) | Thin, legalistic coverage. |
| "Ransomware small business what to do first" | Vendor-heavy. |

### Who wins today and how

- Government (FTC, IRS, FBI, CISA): authority and rankings, but prose written for compliance, not for someone holding a phone.
- AARP: trusted with the 50-plus audience, strong helpline, articles are long and ad-supported.
- Aura, Norton, Guard.io and affiliate sites: fast, SEO-tuned, and every page ends in a product. Most "voice cloning scam" results in October 2026 were vendor blogs pushing detection apps.
- Reddit r/scams and YouTube: the best plain-English answers, but unsourced and slow at the moment of panic.
- Consumer Reports Security Planner (Citizen Lab 2017, Consumer Reports since October 2020): the closest thing to the Path, with 48 recommendations after its 2023 redesign and an embeddable API planned for late 2025 (Consumer Reports Innovation Lab). It is individual-only, pre-AI in framing, and offers no "it just happened" path and no business version.

### Hero products that drove traffic for comparable brands

- Have I Been Pwned: a free lookup with one input and one honest answer. Troy Hunt reported a 57,000 percent traffic rise after the Ashley Madison breach (2015); third-party estimates put it at about 6 million visits in August 2026 (Semrush, August 2026, estimate). The lesson: one free tool, one question, one answer, no upsell, and the press cites it every time there is a breach.
- Security Planner: a quiz that returns a plan. Proof that the Path's shape works, and that a nonprofit could not make it famous.
- Wirecutter's "the one we'd buy" and Consumer Reports' tests: the brand is the verdict. People share verdicts, not articles.

The gap, stated plainly: nobody owns the honest, calm, sourced answer to "is this real, and what do I do in the next two minutes," for the exact message in someone's hand, with nothing to sell.

## 2. Three candidate angles

### Angle A: "Is this real?" The panic-moment answer engine

- Positioning line: When something feels off, this is the calm place to check.
- Hero product: a free checker that works like Have I Been Pwned for scams. One screen: "What just happened?" with chips (a text about a toll, a package, my bank; a call from a grandchild, the IRS, my boss; I clicked a link; I sent money; I shared a code). Each answer gives a verdict as a procedure, not a detection claim: what this is, the next two minutes, who to tell, and a card to send the family. Every verdict page is its own indexable URL and carries the FTC and IC3 figures with dates.
- Why it would be shared: the output is a card someone forwards to the family group chat or posts on Facebook, where the losses start (FTC, April 2026). The brand becomes the thing you send your mother.
- Risk: the space is full of vendor blogs. Winning means being faster to the exact message type (toll, package, bank) and more honest (we never claim to detect a fake). It also needs seasonal discipline: tax-season pages live by January.

### Angle B: The Security Planner for the AI era

- Positioning line: Your digital life, in order. (The current front door.)
- Hero product: the Path, with its 132 steps published as indexable pages and the end states leading each stage.
- Why it would be shared: people share progress and plans less than they share warnings. It is the retention product, not the acquisition product.
- Risk: Consumer Reports already has this shape and brand. Setup-moment search is smaller and slower than panic-moment search. On its own, this angle grows slowly.

### Angle C: The small-business AI rulebook

- Positioning line: The rules your team needs before AI, deepfakes and changed bank details cost you money.
- Hero product: a free one-page AI rule generator (the AI Acceptable Use Policy, without the account wall) and a free two-channel payment verification kit, with the Documentation Pack as the paid next step.
- Why it would be shared: owners and office managers share templates on LinkedIn; accountants hand them to clients.
- Risk: lower volume. It is the monetization lane, not the traffic lane.

## 3. Recommendation

Lead with Angle A as the traffic engine, keep Angle B as the product people stay for, and run Angle C as the business lane that pays. They are one funnel: the checker answers the panic moment, every verdict ends with "now make sure it can't happen again" and starts the Path with the matching chip, and the business verdicts end at the free AI rule and the Documentation Pack.

### The 90-day traffic thesis

Own the exact-message searches that government pages answer badly and vendor blogs answer dishonestly. Each verdict page targets one message type as people type it, opens with a two-sentence answer, carries dated FTC and IC3 figures, and ends with a share card and a Path chip. Tax season starts in January, so the IRS verdicts ship first.

Targets for the 90 days, in order:
1. Ship the checker on the homepage navbar as "Something just happened?" and at its own URL, with the ten home verdicts and six business verdicts from the demand map. Each verdict is a page.
2. Ship the share card: a one-screen image and a printable version, generated per verdict, with the mark and the phrase "Hang up. Call back on a number you already have."
3. Submit everything to Search Console, and enable analytics so the 80 percent stops being a guess.
4. Publish the IRS, toll and bank-text verdicts by January 5 and refresh them when the FTC's annual data lands in March.
5. Measure one number: verdict pages visited, and the share of those that click a Path chip.

### What to build first

The checker with the first six verdicts: toll text, package text, bank fraud-alert text, grandchild or family voice call, "I clicked a link," and "vendor changed bank details" for business. These six cover the largest reported categories and the ones the site already has sourced material for (voice cloning, AI phishing and BEC, two-channel verification, the safe word and callback rule).

## 4. Staying true to the brand

- Independence: the checker sells nothing and recommends no product. A verdict names a free action (call the number on your card, freeze your credit, report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov or ic3.gov). When a tool helps, it is one from the toolkit with the free option beside it, as the Path already does.
- Calm voice: a verdict is a procedure, never a diagnosis of the person's gullibility and never a claim to detect fakes. The tone is "this is common, here is the order to do things in."
- Every number sourced: the verdicts are the most-cited pages on the site, so they inherit the marker-and-source rules and show "reviewed on" dates.
- The Path stays the core product: the checker is the door, the Path is the house. Every verdict ends by starting the Path with the matching chip, so the 80 percent arrive in a panic and stay for the plan.
- Sovereignty stays the "take control" layer the owner wants it to be, reachable from the end of every Path stage.

## 5. Stop doing, or deprioritize

- Stop treating the Controls Library and the industry briefs as public destinations. They support the audit; they do not bring anyone in.
- Stop expanding the government-contractor track. One step that points to the right authority is enough.
- Stop selling the Ongoing Protection subscription until its recurring value exists, as the roadmap already says.
- Deprioritize the AI-versus-SI stance as a marketing object. Keep it, but it is an inside conversation.
- Deprioritize landscape-style threat articles ("the state of AI scams"). Vendor blogs own that format; exact-message verdicts beat it.
- Do not build a detection tool of any kind. The honest position, backed by the sources above, is that people cannot reliably tell a cloned voice by ear, and the brand's credibility rests on saying so.
