# Five-year value roadmap: what members will pay for, 2027 to 2031

Written 2026-10-27 by the strategy role. The evidence behind every number here is in `ai-era-evidence-2026.md` (six research tracks, every figure sourced and dated); this document cites the original source in brackets and leaves the links to the evidence file. Scope was set by the owner: anything the two of us can build, including installable software, hardware and institutional partnerships; hosted member data is allowed; follow the value wherever it is; no referral, affiliate or sponsorship money, ever.

This is not a plan for next quarter. The existing plans (`implementation-plan.md` milestones 2 and 3, `membership-implementation.md`, `marketing-plan.md`) stand. This is the answer to a different question: what will the threats look like in 2028 and 2031, and what should we be building now so that a member in those years says "this is the only thing that actually helped." Section 4 ends with the year-one build order.

**Revised 2026-10-27 after an outside review.** The first draft shipped something from five bets in 2027, which two people cannot do well. This revision narrows year one to six pieces of software, starts the Line as a card and a directory rather than a phone system, makes the Pause's first phase a year-one product, gives Business its evidence kit in year one, sets the Desk's human window at the next business day, keeps the Standing mark private until a broker and counsel have reviewed it, and turns the Vault into an export. The bets themselves are unchanged.

## 1. The thesis

The AI era does not bring households and small firms new kinds of attacks. It removes the labor ceiling from the attacks they already know, and aims them at the two places no product covers: the phone call and the recovery path. Reported US losses hit $20.9 billion in 2025, up 26% in a year, doubling roughly every three years, with the growth concentrated in single six-figure payments that the victim authorized, mostly by people over 60 (FBI IC3, April 2026; FTC, March 2026). Voice cloning is free from a few seconds of audio (Consumer Reports, March 2025). People identify cloned voices at or below chance (International AI Safety Report, 2026). AI-written lures beat elite human red teams in March 2025 (Hoxhunt, April 2025). Scam operations now run thousands of AI personas and voice bots that pre-screen victims for human closers (Europol IOCTA 2026; Anthropic, September 2026).

At the same time, people have connected AI agents to their email (36% of agent users), browsers (33%) and financial accounts (20%) while those agents complete under 1% of online orders (Menlo Ventures, September 2026; First Page Sage, October 2026). Every shipped class of agent has a reproduced hijack, and OpenAI and the UK NCSC say prompt injection may never be fully solved (TechCrunch, December 2025). The exposure has arrived before the convenience, and there is no product anywhere that lets a family or a five-person firm see and limit what their agents can reach, spend and remember.

Two conclusions follow, and the whole roadmap rests on them.

**Detection is dead as a household control.** Every authority that used to teach spotting now prescribes procedure: a family word, a callback to a known number, limits set in advance, no standing permissions (FBI PSA, December 2024; Google Cloud forecast, November 2025; OpenAI, December 2025). Our brand already says this. The roadmap turns it from advice into infrastructure.

**The market sells monitoring and insurance; it does not sell notice, ritual, help or governance.** Across complaints, regulator surveys and reviews, the unmet asks are the same five: a human who notices early, a verification ritual set up once and rehearsed, help at the moment of an incident including nights and weekends, an independent source with no affiliate motive, and a way to govern AI tools at home and in a small firm. Nobody funded sells any of them (evidence report, section on competitors). The gap between the free helpline and the $5,000-a-month retainer is empty. The gap between Microsoft 365 Business Premium and a $1,500-a-month vCISO is empty. Those two empty bands are where the next five years of member value live.

The current membership sells an answer (Ask) and a plan (the Path). Both are read. Consumer Reports, with 4 million members, is losing up to 30% of its traffic to AI answers and its members are mostly over 55 (What's New in Publishing, March 2026). A membership that is used rather than read survives that. Every bet below is a service with state: it holds something for the member, watches something, or answers at a moment when nothing else does.

Three things in this table are structural, not seasonal. Live voice and video will stay outside every verification layer through 2031, so the callback ritual is the durable control for the whole period, not a stopgap. The losses that keep growing are the ones the victim authorizes, which no detector touches and only a rule about where money can go, agreed in advance, would have stopped: the FTC finds that 5% of older adults' reports carry 68% of their losses (FTC, December 2025). ## 3. The seven bets

Each bet is a service with state. Each is named for what it does for the member, in the brand's plain voice; the names are working titles for the owner to keep or change. Each lists what it is, why it wins, what it needs from us, and when. Bets build on one another in the order given.

### Bet 0: Circles, the member unit

**What it is.** The membership stops being one login and becomes a circle: everyone at the address and the people you look out for, with roles. A viewer sees progress. An approver gets asked before money moves. A trusted contact is who the Line calls. An adult child buys; a parent uses; a sibling watches without taking over. One business circle holds the owner, whoever got handed IT, and the accountant who answers the insurer's form.

**Why it wins.** Every elder-protection product that works is sold to the adult child and used by the parent (Carefull through 35+ banks; EverSafe's "trusted advocates"). The r/Scams case of the mother who lost about $1 million drew only non-product answers to "how do we watch without control of her accounts" (The Cooldown on r/Scams). AARP's free second household member is the cheapest retention device in the category. The Path's two-person steps (the safe word, the card by the phone, the elder conversation) already require a second person; today that person has no account.

**What it needs.** A `circles` table and role model on top of `path_profiles`, invitations by email or printed code, per-role views of the Path and progress counts, and a consent record for every role that can see or approve anything. No new content. This is the foundation every later bet stands on, so it comes first.

**When.** 2027, first half.

### Bet 1: The Line, verification as infrastructure

**What it is.** A callback that has already been set up for you, in two phases.

Phase one is a card and a directory. Every circle keeps a trusted directory, saved in calm conditions: the bank's number from the back of the card, the grandchild's real number, the IT provider's main line. The card by the phone carries the rule and those numbers: "If anyone calls about money, a code, or an emergency: hang up and call a number on this card." In the app the same directory is one tap: call the bank, or call your trusted contact, who sees that the call came from the callback screen. **Check with us** rides alongside: a text number and an email address per circle; forward the message, get the verdict and the script in a minute from the same corpus Ask uses, and if it turns out to be real trouble, the message opens a Desk case.

Phase two is the Line itself, and it ships only after phase one has been used and counsel has signed off. A phone number that belongs to the circle answers (an AI voice, announced as such, inbound only, recording disclosed), asks who the caller claimed to be, dials the real number from the directory, announces itself and bridges the call, or pings the trusted contact and holds. The caller never gets to be the one who chose the number. Every call leaves a one-line record in the circle: who called, who was reached, what was decided. **Drills** come with it: once a quarter, with the member's prior consent recorded in the app, a practice call so the first time Grandma uses the callback rule is not during a real scam.

**Why it wins.** The callback is the one control the FBI, Google, OpenAI and Europol all prescribe, and nobody sells it. It works however good the fake gets, which is the only honest position our brand can take. Apple's and Google's free call screening cannot help with "grandchild in jail" because it screens before the voice, not after (PIRG, 2025). The only consumer product that listens during a live call is a seed-stage app that could not screen calls at launch (SecurityBrief, July 2026), and it makes a detection claim we never will. The Line does not judge the voice. It changes who dials.

**What it needs.** Phase one needs only the circle's directory screen, the printed card and the existing Ask corpus. Phase two needs inbound-only telephony (Twilio or Telnyx at under a cent a minute; a transcribed, model-assisted call runs $0.04 to $0.13 a minute, so a thousand members making one five-minute call a month costs a few hundred dollars), a trusted-directory UI in the circle, number ownership on a signing carrier for A-level attestation, the recording announcement, and counsel on two points: the FCC's February 2024 ruling requires prior express consent before any outbound call that uses an AI voice (drills are outbound, so consent is captured in-app and re-confirmed by the Line itself), and 11 to 13 states require all-party consent to record. An Android app can later hold the call-screening role and offer the callback as the phone rings; iOS gives only block lists and an unknown-sender filter, so on iPhone the Line stays a number on a card and in Contacts. Print and ship the card: a physical object by the phone is the retention device the learning report asked for.

**When.** Check with us in 2027, first half, on the existing Ask corpus. The directory, the card and one-tap calling ship with Circles. Before any telephony we test the workflow by hand: for the first circles that opt in, Check with us answers "someone called saying they were my bank" with the number from that circle's own directory and a one-tap call to the trusted contact. If members use the card and the directory, phase two is worth building; if they do not, a phone system will not fix it. Phase two, the number with bridging and drills, in 2028 if the hand test shows use and counsel has cleared consent and recording. The Android app with the call-screening role in 2029.

### Bet 2: The Pause, money rules with a second person

**What it is.** A pre-committed rule, written by the member in calm conditions, about where money may go and who has to say yes. "Nothing over $2,000 to a new payee without Sarah." "No crypto, gift cards or instant transfers to anyone I met online." The rule lives in the circle and on a wallet card. When the member is about to move money, they open the Pause: it asks who, why and how, starts a cool-off clock (an hour, a day, their choice), and pings the approver, who sees the same screen and says yes, no, or "call me." If the member later tells the Line or the Desk "I think I was scammed," the Pause record is the evidence pack: what was said, when, to whom.

Phase two connects to the bank. With the member's consent, the Pause ingests the bank's own transaction and payee alerts (the same read-only feeds Carefull and EverSafe use) and prompts the approver when a large transfer or a new payee appears. The promise has to be worded carefully: a read-only feed usually sees a transfer after it has been started, so phase two makes the second person fast and cannot stop a payment. Stopping one before it leaves takes the bank, which is phase three. In phase three, where a bank or credit union licenses the program, the Pause becomes the "trusted contact" flow the bank shows its own customers, under our name, with no bounty per sign-up.

**Why it wins.** The dollar harm is in authorized payments, and the limits layer alone would remove most of it (FTC, December 2025: 5% of reports, 68% of losses). The UK's bank-pays regime cut APP fraud 21% and reimbursed 88% of in-scope losses (PSR, October 2025; Frontier Economics, July 2026); the US has no such rule and will not by 2031 unless a state attorney general wins one (New York's Zelle suit survived dismissal in July 2026). Until liability moves, the household's only protection is a rule it set itself. Carefull monitors after the fact at $29.99 a month and has no approval step; nobody productizes the two-person rule.

**What it needs.** Circles with the approver role; a rule editor that writes plain sentences; the cool-off and ping; a printable wallet card. Phase two needs an aggregator connection (Plaid-class, read-only), which is the first hosted member data we hold beyond the Path, so it needs the privacy page rewritten and a deletion path tested before launch. Phase three needs one credit union willing to pay a flat annual program fee.

**When.** Phase one in 2027, second half, with no bank connection. It is the year's largest harm reduction for the least build: once Circles exists it is mostly copy and one screen. We run the approver flow with the first circles and watch whether approvers answer inside the cool-off. Phase two in 2028 if the owner chooses it (section 9). Phase three in 2029, only if a credit union wants to take part.

### Bet 3: The Ledger, governing the household's and the firm's AI

**What it is.** A register of every AI assistant and agent in the circle: which product, whose account, what it can reach (mail, files, calendar, bank, browser), what it may spend, what it remembers, and who owns it. For a home, the Ledger is the "first AI" setup every teen and every grandparent gets, the way a first phone gets a setup. For a firm, it is the agent register the AI Acceptable Use Policy already implies, with a named owner per agent, as Forrester prescribes for enterprises (June 2026) and nobody writes for a five-person office.

Phase one is guided: a monthly review routine built from the Path's AI stage (`ai-settings`, `ai-connected-apps`, `ai-approval-rule`, `money-agent-spending`), with the "never all three" rule (private data, untrusted content, a way to send) applied to each agent and a red card when an agent has all three. Phase two is live: with the member's consent, the Ledger reads the OAuth grants on their Google and Microsoft accounts (read-only scopes), lists which apps and agents hold standing tokens, flags the Drift-shaped risk (a chat agent holding a standing token into the CRM was how 700+ organizations were breached in August 2025), and shows drift week to week. Phase three is enforcement: a member-run gateway (open-source MCP gateways exist; Anthropic's Agent SDK exposes a permission pipeline with a human-approval hook) through which the circle's agents reach tools, enforcing the member's own House Rules: no standing permission to send, buy, share or delete; spend caps that mirror the signed mandates the card networks are standardizing; a log every member can read. The House Rules file is published as a small open format we maintain, so a vendor that wants to honor a family's limits can read them without us in the loop.

**Why it wins.** The governance surface is empty everywhere: ChatGPT's and Gemini's parental controls give "zero visibility into individual conversations" and nothing on actions or spending; no parental-control suite mentions AI memory; the standards are enterprise-scoped and not final before 2027; agent identity products from Microsoft and Okta assume a corporate directory (evidence report, agent era section). Gartner's own line is that 80% of unauthorized agent transactions through 2028 will be internal policy violations, not attacks, which is exactly what a register and a rule prevent. Agent users are the paying users: 92% of them already pay for AI (Menlo, September 2026). And memory is the sovereignty flashpoint: ChatGPT offers no export of the synthesized profile, and portability will reach consumers through EU rules before standards (Microsoft preprint, May 2026). The Ledger is the Digital Sovereignty pillar made into a product.

**What it needs.** Phase one is content plus a small register UI. Phase two needs Google and Microsoft OAuth app review for read-only directory and token-listing scopes, which takes months and a privacy policy that passes review; start the review in 2028 for a 2029 ship. Phase three needs a hosted gateway with per-circle keys and a careful threat model: we become part of the member's agent trust chain, and we must be honest that a gateway reduces blast radius and cannot stop injection. The House Rules format needs a one-page spec and a reference parser.

**When.** Phase one in early 2028; until then the Path's AI stage carries the guidance. Phase two in 2029, after OAuth review. Phase three is a research bet, not a commitment: it puts us inside the member's agent trust chain and may mean holding keys, so it waits for the FIDO agent-payment specifications, a security review and evidence that members want it. 2030 at the earliest.

### Bet 4: The Desk, help that stays with you

**What it is.** An incident desk that sits in the empty band between the free weekday helpline and the $5,000-a-month retainer. A member who says "something happened" gets, in this order: a procedure now (the Threat Check verdict, extended into a case), a case file that holds state (what happened, when, who was told, every clock running), the letters and requests already written (the bank recall request inside Coalition's 72-hour window, the Regulation E dispute, the credit-bureau fraud alert, the TAKE IT DOWN removal request, the FTC and IC3 reports pre-filled from the case), and a human within a stated window. At Member and Circle prices that window is the next business day: at 9 p.m. the procedure and the case file are there at once, and a person reads the case and calls the next business day. A faster human response is a paid incident upgrade, or part of Standing. Nothing we write implies round-the-clock emergency response. For business members the IR Planner becomes live: "declare an incident" runs their own plan with the real clocks (24 and 72 hours for the EU CRA, 30 days for FTC Safeguards, 72 hours for most insurers, state breach timelines), assigns steps to circle members, and produces the record an insurer or regulator will ask for.

A **Claim Navigator** rides along: the independent map of who actually pays. What each bank, card network, Zelle, carrier and platform reimburses, how to claim, and what to say, kept current from public policies and state actions. In the UK, 71% of victims did not know they could claim and 49% never tried (PSR, October 2025); the US is worse because the rules are voluntary and unpublished. No bank pays us, so we can say which banks pay.

**Why it wins.** AARP's helpline handles 100,000 calls a year for the whole country, weekdays, and explicitly offers "guidance, not investigation or recovery"; Cybercrime Support Network has no phone line; commercial incident response starts at $250 an hour with 40-hour minimums (evidence report, business models section). At a few thousand households, true emergencies are a handful a month, which a founder-plus-roster can cover. The Desk is what the Threat Check already promises and the verdict pages stop short of. It is also the only bet that makes the Documentation Pack's incident plan worth $299 again: a plan that runs is worth more than a plan that prints.

**What it needs.** A `cases` model with timeline, clocks and documents; letter templates per rail and per rule; a review roster (the founder first, then two or three vetted practitioners paid per shift); a statement in the terms that the Desk guides and documents and does not investigate, recover funds or guarantee outcomes, with tech E&O and cyber cover (about $2,500 to $3,500 a year at $1 million limits for a firm like ours, Insureon, June 2026) and a broker conversation before launch about attestation wording. Ask gains a "declare an incident" tool.

**When.** 2027 for the software Desk: cases, timelines, clocks, letters and next-business-day review, fed by Check with us. 2028 for the live IR Planner, the Claim Navigator and the paid incident upgrade. The roster scales with membership.

### Bet 5: The Watch, someone who notices

**What it is.** A scheduled sweep per circle that checks what can be checked and says, most weeks, "nothing changed." Phase one is plain matching: public feeds checked against the circle's own lists, with no connectors and no model reading anything until there is a change to explain. From outside: breach notices matched to the circle's account map; the router, firewall, NAS and camera models the member listed, matched to end-of-life and exploited-vulnerability lists so a "patch wave" lands as "your model, do this today"; backup and account-review reminders on the member's own schedule; for firms, the domain's DMARC and mail authentication, exposed services, and the website's dependencies. With connectors, later: OAuth grant drift (from the Ledger), passkey coverage on the root accounts, backup last-run dates, and the bank alerts from the Pause. Everything arrives as the weekly note the learning report asked for, now per member, with the Path step to do when something did change.

Two companions. **The Footprint**: how much of your voice and face is public (voicemail greeting, podcast, the school fundraiser video), and your relationship graph on people-search sites, because voice samples and family trees are now the binding inputs to a clone scam (FBI, December 2024). **The Removal Program**: an independent, guided data-broker removal with tracking, done by the member with our scripts or by us for the member, with no vendor. Consumer Reports found DIY as effective as the paid services; Mozilla shut Monitor Plus when its vendor's conflict surfaced, and Krebs asked whether any replacement exists that is not "ethically fraught" (Krebs, November 2025). We are the replacement.

**Why it wins.** "A human who notices early" is the first unmet ask in every source, and the BBB record of the biggest consumer bundle is alerts that arrived days late or not at all. The cost is nothing: a 20,000-token snapshot on Haiku 5.5 batch, even run nightly, is about a tenth of a cent; the expensive part is acquiring the snapshot, which the Ledger and the Pause already acquire for their own reasons. No one serves the sub-50 firm on patch waves at all; the NCSC's May 2026 advice is written for organizations with an IT department.

**Where it does not win.** Breach alerts on their own are a commodity: password managers, phone makers and Have I Been Pwned give them away. The Watch keeps members; it will not win them. The parts only we do are the patch-wave alert matched to the devices a member listed and the email-domain checks for firms, so those lead whenever the Watch is described, and the pitch leads with the Desk, the Pause and the card.

**What it needs.** An asset list per circle (the account map and the device list already exist as Path steps), feeds (HIBP's API, CISA's KEV, vendor end-of-life lists, DMARC lookups), a scheduler (Vercel cron and Queues, Supabase pg_cron), the note sender from milestone three, and for the Removal Program a case-by-case opt-out library we maintain. The Footprint needs a careful line: we show what is public, we do not scrape private things.

**When.** 2027 for the outside-in sweep: breaches, device end-of-life and exploited-vulnerability matches, backup reminders and business email-domain checks. 2028 for the Footprint, connector-fed checks and the Removal Program, once the opt-out library is complete.

### Bet 6: Standing, proof you can hand to whoever asks

**What it is.** For a firm: one evidence kit, maintained continuously, that answers every ask at once. The insurer's form, the enterprise customer's questionnaire, the FTC Safeguards elements, the HIPAA security rule as it lands, the CMMC Level 1 self-assessment and SPRS affirmation, the NIS2 supplier questions from an EU customer, the state notices. The kit holds the risk assessment, the asset inventory, the MFA statement, the vendor and standing-token list (from the Ledger), the backup test log (from the Watch), the incident log and clocks (from the Desk), and an annual sign-off page by a named person. Questionnaires are answered from the kit, not from memory. Later, on top of the kit, an annual **Standing mark**. The mark is an attestation, and attestations carry liability, so it stays private, a dated confirmation the firm can share by choice, until a broker and counsel have reviewed the wording. Once reviewed it becomes a dated, public page that says this firm has the five controls (phishing-resistant MFA on email, admin and remote access; tested offsite backups; patched or retired internet-facing devices; a rehearsed payment-verification rule; an agent register with no standing permissions) and when it was last confirmed.

For a household, later and more carefully: a Standing page the circle keeps for itself, a wallet card, and, where a bank or carrier offers a trusted-contact or verified-customer program, the evidence to enroll.

**Why it wins.** Insurers already pay cash for controls (Coalition's MFA retention credit, At-Bay's Microsoft 365 discounts), so attestation has a market value without us taking a cent from anyone. The UK's Cyber Essentials issued 61,430 certificates in the year to June 2026, up 20%, from £320, driven by customer confidence and contract requirements (gov.uk, 2026). There is no US equivalent for the five-person firm: compliance platforms start at $7,500 a year plus an $8,000 audit, and the alternative is "hire an IT provider." The Cyber Audit and the Policy Library already generate most of the kit; Standing is what they become when they stop being a one-time document.

**What it needs.** A kit model that maps evidence to each framework's asks (the Controls Library, no longer a public destination, becomes this map), questionnaire import and answer drafting from the kit, the annual confirmation flow, and the mark page with a verification link. E&O wording that says Standing attests that the firm confirmed these controls on this date, not that the firm is secure; a broker must bless the words before the first mark. A household Standing waits until a bank or carrier program exists to enroll in.

**When.** The private evidence kit in 2027, second half, built from the Cyber Audit and the Documentation Pack. Business needs something new in year one, and this is the strongest revenue idea in the roadmap. Questionnaire answering in 2028. The public Standing mark no earlier than 2029, after the legal and insurance review. Household Standing only if a bank or carrier program asks for it.

### Bet 7: The Vault, what you keep when the vendor changes

**Near term, an export.** Before any vault, the member gets a continuity packet: one export, from data we already hold, of the circle's account map, recovery kit, trusted directory, money rules, AI register and legacy wishes. It is in open formats, printable and theirs to keep. It ships in 2028 and asks nothing new of our security.

**What it is.** The sovereignty bet, for 2030 and after. The circle's own store of the things the platforms are starting to lock: the memory profile each AI holds about you (exported where the vendor allows, reconstructed from your own review where it does not, deleted on a schedule you set), your voice and likeness wishes (what may be synthesized, by whom, after you are gone; the NO FAKES right if it passes), your House Rules, your account map and recovery kit, and the signed receipts and mandates from agent purchases that will be the evidence in a dispute. Portable by design: a member who leaves takes the Vault.

**Why it wins.** Memory is the new lock-in and the sharpest sovereignty question; the EU will force portability before standards exist, and a US member with a Vault has the leverage an EU member gets by law. The Path's legacy stage already asks people to write down what should happen to their voice and likeness; by 2030 that is a right with a registry somewhere, and the Vault is where the member's answer lives.

**What it needs.** Encrypted per-circle storage with member-held keys, export in open formats, and the C2PA and mandate verifiers (both free, open-source) to read the receipts. It depends on the Ledger's memory work and on where the standards land, so it is the last bet and the least specified. We do not build our own encrypted store in the near term: member-held keys mean key recovery, and key recovery is a support load two people cannot carry.

**When.** The continuity packet in 2028. The encrypted Vault in 2030 to 2031, only if members ask for it and the export standards exist.

## 6. What we will not build

- **Any detection claim.** No "we can tell if the voice is fake." Every measured human test is at or below chance, the detection tools cannot keep pace with generation, and two vendors now give scam checkers away for free. The Line changes who dials; the Watch notices state; the Desk runs procedure. None of them judges a voice.
- **Monitoring-and-insurance bundles.** The category has converged on one kit with undisclosed renewals and late alerts. We do not sell "$1M insurance" and do not resell credit monitoring; the free credit freeze is a Path step.
- **A data-broker removal vendor.** We run our own Removal Program or guide the member's; we never resell DeleteMe, Incogni or anyone else, and we take nothing from them.
- **Anything that needs a referral, affiliate or sponsorship.** Program licenses and member fees only.
- **A security-operations center, endpoint agents or managed detection.** That is the IT provider's business and the insurer's bundle; the Watch tells a member what to ask their IT provider, and Standing records the answer.
- **Scores and grades for households.** Progress stays counts per outcome. Standing is a dated confirmation of named controls, not a grade.
- **Fear.** No countdowns, no "unprecedented," no "lurking." A patch-wave alert says the model, the fix and the deadline, and nothing else.

## 8. Signals and decision points

The dated things to watch, and what each one changes.

- **IC3's AI line item, each April.** $893 million and 22,364 complaints in the 2025 report, called a floor by the FBI. If the 2026 figure doubles, the Line's phone number and the Pause's bank phase move up a year.
- **The FIDO agent-payment specifications.** Handed over in April 2026 with no dates. When a mandate format is final, the Ledger's gateway enforces it and the Vault stores the receipts; until then, phase three waits.
- **The November 2026 federal "SI" definition proposal** (EO 14434, 60 days from September 29). A vocabulary question for us, not a product one: federal pages will say "SI" while every statute and vendor says "AI." Our stance explainer stays the only place we translate.
- **Colorado and California, January 1, 2027.** Automated-decision notices; Colorado's enforcement is stayed by litigation. If they hold, the business Ledger gains an "AI in hiring, lending or housing" clause and Standing gains a line.
- **HIPAA, July 2027, and CMMC's reform task force.** Each moves what the evidence kit must hold. The kit is built to add a framework without rebuilding.
- **The EU, December 2, 2027.** High-risk obligations push questionnaires down to US suppliers of EU customers; Standing's supplier questions ship before then.
- **New York's Zelle suit, and any state that follows the UK's bank-pays rule.** The single rule that changes what a scammed household recovers more than any product. If a US rule lands, the Claim Navigator becomes the most valuable page we own, and the Pause's bank phase becomes the bank's problem to fund.
- **Google and Microsoft OAuth review outcomes.** They decide whether Ledger phase two is live data or a guided inventory. Start early; have the guided version regardless.
- **The hand tests, through 2027.** Whether members use the card and the directory, and whether approvers answer inside the Pause's cool-off. They decide whether the Line gets a phone system and whether the Pause goes near a bank.
- **The first ten Desk cases.** They tell us whether a founder-plus-roster can hold the next-business-day window and what the paid upgrade should promise.

## 9. Open questions for the owner

Settled in this revision: the Line starts as a card and a directory that work on every phone, and its phone system waits for the hand test and counsel. The Desk's human window is the next business day, with faster response as a paid upgrade.

1. **Names.** Circles, the Line, the Pause, the Ledger, the Desk, the Watch, Standing and the Vault are working titles chosen for plainness. The brand names things by what they do; the owner's ear decides.
2. **How far into money.** The Pause's phase two means holding read-only bank feeds for members. That is new for us and it is where the privacy promise is tested, and a read-only feed can only make the approver faster, never stop a payment. The alternative is to stop at phase one and let a credit union license phase three. Phase one is worth shipping either way.
3. **The bar for the Line's phone system.** A suggested bar: by the end of 2027, a meaningful share of circles holding a card have used the directory or Check with us at least once, and counsel has cleared consent and recording. The owner sets the share.

5. **Program licenses.** Credit unions and employers first, or accountants first? The business lane says accountants, because they already hold the insurer's form. The home lane says credit unions, because Carefull proved the channel. Both can start with one conversation each in 2028.

Everything else in this document is a recommendation the owner can act on in order: Circles first, because every bet stands on it; Check with us and the Desk's software next, because they turn a scare into a case; the Watch's outside-in sweep alongside, because it answers "what did you do for me this month"; the Pause's first phase and the Business evidence kit in the second half; the Line's phone system, bank feeds, live connectors and the public mark only after the hand tests and reviews say yes; the Vault when the standards are.
