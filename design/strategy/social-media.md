# Social media: where to capitalize, and what the product needs first

Written 2026-10-26. Companion to `design/roadmap.md`. Every number below carries a named, dated source; where sources disagree, both are given.

The short version: our two audiences live on Facebook and LinkedIn. Instagram is a secondary channel for the 30-to-49 adult child who is looking out for a parent. X is where the security press and practitioners are, not where our readers are. Start on one platform, with the printable artifacts and the "something just happened" page built first, because a post without a place to land is a post wasted.

## 1. Where our two audiences actually are

### Home: parents, remote workers, and adult children helping an older relative

Pew Research Center's social media fact sheet (published October 19, 2026; survey of 5,511 U.S. adults, January 30 to June 18, 2026, margin of error 1.8 points) gives the share of U.S. adults who use each platform:

| Platform | All adults | 30 to 49 | 50 to 64 | 65 and over |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube | 84% | 91% | 85% | 65% |
| Facebook | 70% | 78% | 71% | 59% |
| Instagram | 51% | 64% | 41% | 21% |
| LinkedIn | 33% | 37% | 32% | 13% |
| X | 25% | 20% | 17% | 7% |

Three readings matter for us:

- The older relative is on Facebook (59% of 65 and over) and almost nowhere else we are considering (Instagram 21%, X 7%).
- The adult child who sets up a parent's phone is 30 to 49, and that group is on Facebook (78%) and Instagram (64%) in large numbers.
- The remote worker overlaps with the LinkedIn audience (37% of 30 to 49).

Facebook is also where the harm is. The FTC's Data Spotlight of April 27, 2026 reports about $2.1 billion in losses to scams that started on social media in 2025, roughly eight times the 2020 figure, with nearly 30% of people who reported losing money saying the scam began on social media. Facebook produced the largest reported losses of any platform, ahead of WhatsApp and Instagram, and every age group except people 80 and over lost more to scams that began on social media than to any other contact method. For people 80 and over, phone calls remained the larger loss channel. That makes Facebook both the place our home audience is and the place where our advice is most relevant.

Meta's own October 2025 cybersecurity-month post points families to AARP's Fraud Watch Network and advises against posting complaints in public forums because scammers watch for them. Both are useful for us: AARP is the established voice we should complement rather than compete with, and the "don't post it publicly" advice is a reason our triage page should be a private, calm place rather than a comment thread.

### Business: owner-operators and whoever got handed IT at 1 to 50 people

The Small Business and Entrepreneurship Council's technology survey (March 2026; 517 owners polled online in February 2026, margin of error about 4.4 points) asked which platforms matter most for income and branding. Facebook led at 62%, then Instagram 43%, YouTube 36%, LinkedIn 29% and TikTok 26%. Owners spend their own marketing time on Facebook and Instagram, so they are reachable there as readers too.

LinkedIn is where the "got handed IT" person goes for professional validation, and where the accountants, bookkeepers, practice managers and IT providers who influence small businesses already are. Pew's 2026 figure of 37% of 30 to 49 year olds on LinkedIn is the general-population floor; professional-services and finance people index higher, though no source in this research gives a small-business-specific LinkedIn figure.

### X

X reaches 25% of adults and only 7% of people 65 and over (Pew, October 2026). It is where security researchers, journalists and practitioners talk, and where people like Rachel Tobac of SocialProof Security demonstrate live social-engineering attacks. That is a source of credibility and press contacts, not a traffic source for our readers. Treat it as a listening and relationships channel.

## 2. Per platform

### Facebook (home audience, and small-business owners as people)

**Post:** one scam, one habit, one artifact. The safe-word card. The "when a call feels wrong" card by the phone. "Hang up and call the number on your card." Posts the adult child can share to a parent's timeline or a family group chat. Short videos of a real phone showing a setting (call screening, carrier scam blocking), filmed in one take, no music.

**Formats that work:** single image with large type and one instruction; short vertical video of a setting being turned on; a carousel that tells one story in four frames; a plain-text post that reads like a neighbor. AARP's Fraud Watch alerts are syndicated into local papers monthly (for example the Devils Lake Journal reprints of AARP's August to October 2026 alerts), which shows the appetite for a calm monthly alert format.

**Avoid:** anything that looks like an ad for a product. Fear words. Reposting news of a loss without a step. Public comment threads where people describe what happened to them (Meta's own advice, October 2025, is that scammers watch those posts).

**Cadence one person can sustain:** two posts a week, plus one reply session a week.

### LinkedIn (business audience and the people who advise them)

**Post:** from a named person, not only the company page. Metricool's analysis of 673,658 posts found personal-profile carousels get 63% higher engagement than company-page carousels. Socialinsider's 2025 data set (1.3 million posts, 16,645 business pages) put native document posts at a 7.00% average engagement rate, ahead of multi-image and video. Oktopost's March 2026 benchmark of more than 1,000 B2B pages gives a median engagement rate of 5.72% and median reach of 826 impressions per post, so the realistic expectation for a new company page is hundreds of views, not thousands, and the founder's profile will outperform it.

**Formats that work:** a document carousel that walks through one Path step ("Verify every payment change on a second channel, with two people") in six frames; a one-page AI rule shown as an image; a plain-text story of an owner who got a changed-bank-details email and what the callback caught.

**Avoid:** vendor-style "threat landscape" posts, statistics without a dated source, anything that reads as selling the Documentation Pack before the reader has had a free win.

**Cadence:** one carousel a week from the founder's profile, reshared by the page, plus comments on five posts a week from accountants, practice managers and IT providers.

### Instagram (the adult child, and owners as people)

**Post:** the same artifacts as Facebook, cut for Reels and Stories: a 20-second "do this with your mom this weekend" video, a carousel of the safe-word card, a Story that links to one Path chip.

**Formats that work:** Reels of a real phone screen, carousels with one sentence per slide, Stories with a link sticker to a single page.

**Avoid:** polished studio production. The brand is calm and real; a kitchen table reads better than a set.

**Cadence:** one Reel and one carousel a week, cross-posted from Facebook with captions trimmed.

### X

**Post:** citations and corrections, replies to researchers and journalists, the original study when it exists. **Avoid:** trying to build an audience. **Cadence:** reply more than you post.

## 3. Five post series from material we already have

**Series 1: The safe word.** One post a month, same card, different family. First post: "Pick a word tonight. Any word. The rule: if a call, a voice note or a video asks anyone in the family for money or a code, you ask for the word. No word, no money. Then hang up and call back on the number you already have. Here's the card for the fridge." Image: the printable safe-word card. Link: the safe word guide.

**Series 2: What we cut.** One post a week, one product category we don't recommend and why, from the Path's "What we cut" content. First post: "You'll see ads for apps that promise to detect deepfake calls. Fakes keep getting better, so a plan that depends on spotting them won't age well. A safe word and a callback don't depend on spotting anything. We take no referral fees, so this isn't a pitch for the other thing." Image: a quiet card with the sentence.

**Series 3: One step, four ways.** A Path step told as "just do it" on Monday, as a story on Thursday. First post (Monday): "Lock the email account that resets everything. Open your main email's security settings. Add a passkey or an authenticator app. Check the recovery phone and email are yours and current. Ten minutes, and it's the step that protects the rest." Thursday: the story telling of the same step from the Path.

**Series 4: The Long View.** Once a month, one sentence about where this is heading, with no date and no countdown. First post: "In a few years an assistant will pay some of your bills. The habit to build now is the one that still works then: nothing moves money without a second check you set up in advance. That's step three on the Money stage of the Path."

**Series 5: The promise.** Quarterly. First post: "Here's the whole business model. Small businesses can buy our documentation pack. That's it. No referral fees, no affiliate links, no sponsors, and every number on the site links to a named, dated source. If a tool is on our list, it's because we'd use it, and there's a free option beside it."

Business variants of series 2, 3 and 5 go on LinkedIn: the one-page AI rule, "verify every payment change on a second channel," and the independence promise written for an owner.

## 4. What the product needs before social is worth it

A post is a door. Today most doors on the site open onto a long page. Build these first, in this order:

1. **Printable artifacts** with their own pages and social cards: the safe-word card, the "when a call feels wrong" card, the one-page AI rule, the "never change payment details by email" notice for clients. These are what people hand to a parent or a boss, and the handoff is the real distribution.
2. **The "something just happened" page** for home and for business. It is the page every scam post should link to, and it is the one most likely to be shared by someone who just had a close call. It must be calm, private (no comments), and three steps deep.
3. **Proper social cards** on every page that will be linked: a specific Open Graph title and image per guide, threat and step, not the shared site image. Facebook and LinkedIn render the card; a generic one looks like an ad.
4. **Indexable step pages** (`/steps/<id>`), so a LinkedIn carousel about one step can link to that step rather than to the quiz.
5. **The first-click-pays Path**, so a chip shared in a post lands on a step, not on eight questions.
6. **A named person.** LinkedIn rewards a face; Facebook trusts a neighbor. The About page needs the author's name, photo and why.
7. **Web Analytics and Search Console**, so we can see which posts bring people who stay.

## 5. Metrics that matter, and the ones to ignore

**Matter:** visits that reach a step or an artifact page; paths started from a social link; artifact downloads and prints; saved paths; replies and messages that describe a situation; mentions by AARP-type organizations, local news and accountants; email sign-ups once the monthly note exists.

**Ignore:** follower counts, impressions, likes on their own, and any engagement-rate benchmark as a target. The LinkedIn benchmarks above range from 2.1% to 21.77% depending on who measured and how, which is a sign they are not a goal to manage to.

## 6. Ranked recommendation

1. **Facebook first.** It is where the older relative is (59% of 65 and over, Pew, October 2026), where the adult child is (78% of 30 to 49), where owners already spend their marketing time (62% rank it most important, SBE Council, March 2026), and where the losses are (largest of any platform, FTC, April 2026). One account, two posts a week, the safe-word series and "what we cut." Encourage sharing into family group chats and local groups rather than running our own group.
2. **LinkedIn second,** from the founder's profile, one carousel a week, for the business path and the advisers who influence it.
3. **Instagram** as a cross-post of the Facebook material, cut for Reels, once the artifacts exist.
4. **X** for listening and relationships only.

Do none of it until items 1 to 3 in section 4 exist. The first month on Facebook should be able to point every post at a card, a page or a step.
