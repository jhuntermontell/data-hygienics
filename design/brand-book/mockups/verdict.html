A call sounded like my child or grandchild and asked for money

Treat it as a scam until you reach them yourself, because AI can copy a voice from a short clip. Hang up and call them on the number you already have before you send anything.

The next two minutes

  1. Hang up, even if they beg you not to. Hurry and secrecy are part of the script.
  2. Call your family member on the number saved in your phone. No answer? Call someone who would be with them.
  3. If your family has a safe word, ask for it. No word, no money.
  4. Never pay by gift card, wire, crypto or cash handed to a courier on a call you didn't start.

Who to tell

  • The rest of your familyTell them it happened, so the next call reaches someone who already knows the rule.

Why the rule holds

A scammer can copy a voice from a short audio clip, often taken from video posted online.1 The FBI's report on 2025 complaints says losses to these "distress" calls that used voice cloning passed $5 million, and that the calls now imitate other relatives and friends, not only grandchildren.2

The FBI recommends a secret word or phrase that only your family knows, and hanging up to call back on a number you look up yourself.3 The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) gives the same advice: reach the person directly before you send money.4

Pressure to keep the call secret, and requests for a wire, crypto, a payment app or gift cards, are signs of a scam. Call another relative or friend even if the caller says not to.5

You never have to judge the voice. Calling back on a number you already have works whether the copy is perfect or not.
